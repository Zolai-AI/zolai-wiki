## 1. How to Read Zolai Texts

### 1.1 Word Order (SOV)

Zolai uses **Subject–Object–Verb (SOV)** word order. The verb always comes last. When reading, scan from left to right but keep the verb in mind — the meaning "lands" on the final verb.

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| S + V | `Pasian om hi.` | God exists. |
| S + O + V | `Gam ka mu hi.` | I see the land. |
| S + IO + DO + V | `Pasian in amaute thupha a pia hi.` | God gave them blessings. |

**Reading tip:** Identify the subject first, then the object, then wait for the verb at the end to complete the meaning.

### 1.2 Particles — The Glue of Zolai

Particles are small words that carry grammatical meaning. They appear in predictable positions:

| Position | Particles | Function |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| After noun (subject) | `in` | Ergative agent marker |
| Before verb | `a`, `ka`, `na` | Person agreement (3SG, 1SG, 2SG) |
| After verb (tense) | `ta`, `zo`, `khin`, `lai`, `ding` | Past, completive, experiential, progressive, future |
| Sentence-final | `hi`, `ve`, `hen`, `un`, `in` | Declarative, informal, polite, imperative, topic |
| Question | `hiam`, `diam` | Yes/no question, rhetorical question |

**Reading tip:** Particles are usually short (1–3 letters). They don't carry dictionary meaning but tell you *how* the sentence relates to time, mood, and participants.

### 1.3 Morphemes — Building Blocks

Zolai is agglutinative — words are built from stems + affixes:

| Component | Examples | Function |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Directional | `hong` (toward), `va` (come), `khia` (go out) | Movement direction |
| Verb stem | `pai` (go), `mu` (see), `ne` (eat), `gen` (know) | Core meaning |
| Aspect | `ta` (past), `zo` (completed), `ding` (future) | Time marking |
| Particle | `hi` (declarative), `hen` (polite), `un` (imperative) | Mood |

**Example breakdown:**
```
Laisiangtho hawmkhia ding.
[lai + siang + tho] [hawm + khia] [ding]
[book + clean + suffix] [print + publish] [FUT]
"The Bible will be published."
```

### 1.4 Sentence-Final Particles — Mood and Tone

The last word of a sentence tells you the speaker's intent:

| Particle | Mood | Example |
|----------|------|---------|
| `hi` | Declarative (neutral) | `Ka pai hi.` (I go.) |
| `ve` | Informal/soft | `Ka pai ve.` (I'm going.) |
| `hen` | Polite request | `Hong pai hen.` (Please come.) |
| `un` | Imperative | `Pai un.` (Go!) |
| `in` | Topic/focus | `Tua in...` (That one...) |
| `hiam` | Question | `Na pai hiam?` (Do you go?) |
| `diam` | Rhetorical question | `Na lung a kim diam?` (Is your heart not heavy?) |

## 2. Reading Strategies by Text Type

### 2.1 News Articles (Zomidaily)

**Characteristics:**
- Formal register (`hi`, not `ve`)
- Frequent use of `ciangin` (therefore), `ahih manin` (because)
- Ergative `in` with organizational agents
- Loan words: `copyright`, `vitamin`, `quality`, `technology`

**Strategy:**
1. **Skim for topic sentence** — usually the first sentence with `ciangin` or `ahih manin`
2. **Identify organizations** — look for `... in` (ergative agent)
3. **Watch for loan words** — often appear in parentheses or after a Zolai explanation
4. **Check tense** — `ta` (past) for events, `ding` (future) for plans

**Example news opening:**
```
Zomite te zolai siamna panpihna sungah hong kipan ahih ciangin,
copyright ngaih hiam? zomite te thu a siam hi.
```
**Reading:** The Zomi people... copyright... therefore... the Zomi people know the matter.

### 2.2 Devotional / Religious Texts

**Characteristics:**
- Biblical register with `hi` (never `ve`)
- Frequent religious vocabulary: `Pasian` (God), `Topa` (Lord), `Kumpipa` (Savior)
- Quotative speech: `... a ci hi` (he said)
- Hymn-like rhythm in poetry sections

**Strategy:**
1. **Identify speech verbs** — `ci hi` (said), `pau hi` (spoke), `thu hi` (told)
2. **Track quotation marks** — speech often enclosed in `...` or introduced by `ci`
3. **Look for blessing/cursing patterns** — `thupha` (blessing), `kangaih` (suffering)
4. **Connect to Bible context** — many references assume biblical knowledge

**Example devotional passage:**
```
Pasian in vantung leh leitung a piangsak hi.
Amah in mite leh numei a bawl hi.
Amah in amaute thupha a pia hi.
```
**Reading:** God created heaven and earth. He made people male and female. He gave them blessings.

### 2.3 Personal Narratives

**Characteristics:**
- Mixed register (formal + informal)
- First person: `kei ka` (I), `ka` (I)
- Emotional particles: `mahmah` (very), `lua` (too much), `dih` (just)
- Direct speech mixed with narration

**Strategy:**
1. **Follow the timeline** — look for `ciangin` (then), `tepen` (today), `napi` (yesterday)
2. **Identify emotions** — `lungdam` (joy), `lungkham` (worry), `lungngai` (longing)
3. **Track speaker changes** — first person vs quoted speech
4. **Note informal particles** — `ve`, `hia`, `lua` indicate casual tone

**Example personal narrative:**
```
Kei ka Tedim ah ka pai ta hi.
Ciangin ka nu leh ka pa kiton ta hi.
Lungdam mahmah ka hi.
```
**Reading:** I went to Tedim. Then I met my mother and father. I was very happy.

### 2.4 Academic / Grammar Texts

**Characteristics:**
- Highly formal (`hi`, never `ve`)
- Technical terminology with Zolai equivalents
- Frequent use of `pen` (is/that is), `ahi hi` (is)
- Structured with numbered lists and examples

**Strategy:**
1. **Look for definitions** — `... pen ...` (X is Y)
2. **Identify examples** — often preceded by `ahih ciangin` (therefore) or `nam in` (for example)
3. **Track grammatical terms** — `kammal` (word), `lungkham` (phrase), `thu` (sentence)
4. **Read examples carefully** — they illustrate the rule being discussed

**Example academic passage:**
```
Zolai thu kammal pen Zomite minam thu ahi hi.
Amah in Sino-Tibetan kai pen hi.
```
**Reading:** The Zolai language is the language of the Zomi people. It belongs to the Sino-Tibetan family.

## 3. Vocabulary Guessing Strategies

### 3.1 Compound Word Decomposition

Many Zolai words are compounds. Break them apart to guess meaning:

| Compound | Parts | Guess | Actual Meaning |
|----------|-------|-------|----------------|
| `vantung` | `van` (sky) + `tung` (top) | sky-top | heaven |
| `leitung` | `lei` (ground) + `tung` (above) | ground-above | earth |
| `laisiangtho` | `lai` (book) + `siang` (clean) + `tho` | book-clean-? | Bible |
| `pasian` | `pa` (father) + `sian` (great) | great father | God |
| `tapa` | `ta` (beginning) + `pa` (father) | first father | son/life |
| `lungdam` | `lung` (heart) + `dam` (well) | heart-well | joy, healthy |
| `lungkham` | `lung` (heart) + `kham` (blocked) | heart-blocked | anxiety |
| `hotkhiatna` | `hot` (save) + `khiat` (free) + `na` | save-free-ness | salvation |

### 3.2 Cognate Recognition

Zolai shares vocabulary with related Chin languages:

| Zolai | Hakha | Falam | Paite | Meaning |
|-------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| `pasian` | `pathian` | `pathian` | `pasian` | God |
| `gam` | `ram` | `ram` | `gam` | earth |
| `tapa` | `fapa` | `fapa` | `tapa` | son/life |
| `topa` | `bawipa` | `bawipa` | `topa` | Lord |

### 3.3 Loan Word Detection

English, Malay, and Burmese loan words often appear in modern texts:

| Loan Word | Origin | Zolai Context |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| `copyright` | English | Legal texts, modern articles |
| `vitamin` | English | Health articles |
| `quality` | English | Business articles |
| `technology` | English | Education articles |
| `computer` | English | Modern vocabulary |
| `internet` | English | Modern vocabulary |
| `education` | English | Formal/academic |
| `government` | English | Political texts |

### 3.4 Context Clues

| Clue Type | Strategy | Example |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| Repetition | Word appears multiple times = important | `Pasian` in devotional text |
| Parallelism | Same structure repeated with different words | `Ka ... hi. Na ... hi. A ... hi.` |
| Contrasts | Opposite words near each other | `hoih` (good) vs `hoihsak` (bad) |
| Numbers | Counting words give context | `khat` (one), `ni` (two), `thum` (three) |
| Punctuation | Question marks, exclamation points | `hiam?` = question |

### 3.5 Dictionary Lookup Strategy

1. **Identify the stem** — strip particles (`hi`, `ta`, `ding`, `in`)
2. **Check for compounds** — split on common boundaries (`-tung`, `-siang`, `-na`)
3. **Search in ZO→EN dictionary** — 93,931 entries
4. **Search in EN→ZO dictionary** — 112,220 entries
5. **Check Bible corpus** — 31,102 verses for usage examples

## 4. Common Reading Difficulties and Solutions

### 4.1 Difficulty: Verb at the End

**Problem:** English readers expect SVO, but Zolai is SOV. The verb comes last.

**Solution:** Train yourself to "hold" the subject and object in mind until the verb arrives.

**Practice:**
```
Zomite te zolai siamna panpihna sungah hong kipan ahih ciangin,
copyright ngaih hiam? zomite te thu a siam hi.
```
- Subject: `zomite te` (the Zomi people)
- Object: `copyright ngaih hiam?` (what is copyright?)
- Verb: `siam hi` (know)

### 4.2 Difficulty: Particles Without Dictionary Meaning

**Problem:** Particles like `in`, `a`, `ka`, `na`, `hi`, `ve` don't appear in dictionaries.

**Solution:** Learn particle positions and functions separately.

| Position | Particles | Remember |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| After proper noun subject | `in` | Ergative agent |
| Before verb | `a`, `ka`, `na` | 3SG, 1SG, 2SG agreement |
| After verb | `ta`, `zo`, `khin`, `lai`, `ding` | Tense/aspect |
| Sentence-final | `hi`, `ve`, `hen`, `un` | Mood |

### 4.3 Difficulty: Polysemy (Multiple Meanings)

**Problem:** One word has many meanings depending on context.

**Solution:** Use context to narrow down:

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 | Meaning 3 |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| `dam` | well/healthy | sound (of voice) | firm/stable |
| `sing` | tree | wooden thing | to rise |
| `khua` | village | place | home |
| `ni` | day/sun | two | fire |
| `man` | price | true/correct | as much as |

**Context clue:** `Ka dam hi.` = I am well (health context). `Sing ka mu hi.` = I see a tree (nature context).

### 4.4 Difficulty: Formal vs Informal Register

**Problem:** Same meaning, different words in formal vs informal.

**Solution:** Learn register pairs:

| Formal | Informal | Meaning |
|--------|----------|---------|
| `hi` | `ve` | declarative |
| `hiam` | `hia` | question |
| `ciangin` | `manin` | therefore |
| `ahih ciangin` | `ahih manin` | because |

### 4.5 Difficulty: Unknown Words

**Problem:** You encounter a word not in your vocabulary.

**Solution:** Apply guessing strategies in order:
1. Decompose compound (`vantung` = `van` + `tung`)
2. Check cognates (Hakha/Falam/Paite)
3. Look for loan words (English/Malay)
4. Use surrounding context
5. Consult dictionary (last resort)

## 5. Practice Texts with Glosses and Questions

### Text 1: Simple Greeting (A1)

**Zolai:**
```
Na dam na? Ka dam hi. Lungdam.
```

**Gloss:**
```
[Na] [dam] [na]? [Ka] [dam] [hi.] [Lungdam.]
[2SG] [well] [Q]? [1SG] [well] [DECL.] [Thank you.]
"How are you? I am well. Thank you."
```

**Questions:**
1. What does `dam` mean in this context?
2. What is the question marker?
3. What is the declarative particle?

**Answers:**
1. Well/healthy
2. `na` (question particle after adjective)
3. `hi`

### Text 2: Bible Verse (A2)

**Zolai:**
```
Pasian in vantung leh leitung a piangsak hi.
```

**Gloss:**
```
[Pasian] [in] [vantung] [leh] [leitung] [a] [piangsak] [hi]
[God] [ERG] [heaven] [and] [earth] [3SG.AGR] [create] [DECL]
"God created the heaven and earth."
```

**Questions:**
1. What is the ergative marker and what does it do?
2. What does `leh` mean?
3. What does the `a` before the verb indicate?

**Answers:**
1. `in` — marks the agent of a transitive verb
2. `leh` = and
3. `a` = 3rd person singular agreement marker

### Text 3: Personal Narrative (B1)

**Zolai:**
```
Kei ka Tedim ah ka pai ta hi.
Ciangin ka nu leh ka pa kiton ta hi.
Lungdam mahmah ka hi.
Amah te ka ne ta hi.
```

**Gloss:**
```
[Kei] [ka] [Tedim] [ah] [ka] [pai] [ta] [hi.]
[1SG] [1SG.AGR] [Tedim] [LOC] [1SG.AGR] [go] [PAST] [DECL]
"I went to Tedim."

[Ciangin] [ka] [nu] [leh] [ka] [pa] [ki-ton] [ta] [hi.]
[Then] [1SG.AGR] [mother] [and] [1SG.AGR] [father] [REFL-meet] [PAST] [DECL]
"Then I met my mother and father."

[Lungdam] [mahmah] [ka] [hi.]
[Joy] [very] [1SG.AGR] [DECL]
"I was very happy."

[Amah] [te] [ka] [ne] [ta] [hi.]
[3PL] [PL] [1SG.AGR] [eat] [PAST] [DECL]
"I ate with them."
```

**Questions:**
1. What does `ciangin` mean and what register is it?
2. What does `ki-ton` mean? What does the `ki-` prefix indicate?
3. What does `mahmah` do to the adjective `lungdam`?

**Answers:**
1. `ciangin` = then/therefore (formal register)
2. `ki-ton` = met (reciprocal action); `ki-` is the reflexive/reciprocal prefix
3. `mahmah` = very (intensifier)

### Text 4: News Article (B2)

**Zolai:**
```
Zomite te zolai siamna panpihna sungah hong kipan ahih ciangin,
copyright ngaih hiam? zomite te thu a siam hi.
Tua manin zomite te innkuan leh minam it sungah hong om ahih ciangin,
copyright zomite te thu a siam lo hi.
```

**Gloss:**
```
[Zomite] [te] [zolai] [siamna] [panpihna] [sungah] [hong] [kipan]
[Zomi] [PL] [Zolai] [knowledge] [development] [in/within] [TOWARD] [begin]

[ahih] [ciangin,] [copyright] [ngaih] [hiam?] [zomite] [te] [thu]
[that is] [therefore] [copyright] [what] [Q?] [Zomi] [PL] [matter]

[a] [siam] [hi.]
[3SG.AGR] [know] [DECL]

[Tua] [manin] [zomite] [te] [innkuan] [leh] [minam] [it]
[That] [because] [Zomi] [PL] [household] [and] [nation] [love]

[sungah] [hong] [om] [ahih] [ciangin,] [copyright] [zomite] [te]
[in/within] [TOWARD] [exist] [that is] [therefore] [copyright] [Zomi] [PL]

[thu] [a] [siam] [lo] [hi.]
[matter] [3SG.AGR] [know] [NEG] [DECL]
```

**Translation:**
"Since the Zomi people began Zolai language development, what is copyright? The Zomi people know the matter. Because the Zomi people exist within household and national love, the Zomi people do not know about copyright."

**Questions:**
1. What does `ahih ciangin` mean? What register is it?
2. What does `lo` do in the last sentence?
3. Why does the author use `copyright` in Zolai text?

**Answers:**
1. `ahih ciangin` = therefore/that is why (formal register)
2. `lo` = negation particle (standalone, no `a` agreement)
3. Loan word — no Zolai equivalent exists yet for this modern concept

### Text 5: Devotional Passage (C1)

**Zolai:**
```
Pasian in mite leh numei a bawl hi.
Amah in amaute thupha a pia hi.
"A thu hoih a hi hi," Pasian in a ci hi.
Mite te pasian kibang a om thei kei hi.
Pasian in amaute a ngaih hi a, amaute in Pasian a ngaih hi.
```

**Gloss:**
```
[Pasian] [in] [mite] [leh] [numei] [a] [bawl] [hi.]
[God] [ERG] [people] [and] [female] [3SG.AGR] [create] [DECL]
"God created people and women."

[Amah] [in] [amaute] [thupha] [a] [pia] [hi.]
[3SG] [ERG] [3PL] [blessing] [3SG.AGR] [give] [DECL]
"He gave them blessings."

["A] [thu] [hoih] [a] [hi] [hi,"] [Pasian] [in] [a] [ci] [hi.]
["It] [word] [good] [3SG.AGR] [be] [DECL,"] [God] [ERG] [3SG.AGR] [say] [DECL]
"'It is a good word,' God said."

[Mite] [te] [pasian] [kibang] [a] [om] [thei] [kei] [hi.]
[People] [PL] [God] [same] [3SG.AGR] [exist] [able] [NEG] [DECL]
"People cannot exist the same as God."

[Pasian] [in] [amaute] [a] [ngaih] [hi] [a,]
[God] [ERG] [3PL] [3SG.AGR] [love] [DECL] [and]

[amaute] [in] [Pasian] [a] [ngaih] [hi.]
[3PL] [ERG] [God] [3SG.AGR] [love] [DECL]
"God loved them, and they loved God."
```

**Translation:**
"God created people and women. He gave them blessings. 'It is a good word,' God said. People cannot exist the same as God. God loved them, and they loved God."

**Questions:**
1. What does `kibang` mean? What does the `ki-` prefix indicate?
2. Why is `thei kei hi` used instead of just `kei hi`?
3. Identify the reciprocal pattern in the last two clauses.

**Answers:**
1. `kibang` = same; `ki-` prefix indicates reciprocity/mutuality
2. `thei` = can/able; `thei kei hi` = cannot (ability + negation)
3. Last two clauses: `Pasian in amaute a ngaih hi` (God loved them) + `amaute in Pasian a ngaih hi` (they loved God) — reciprocal love expressed through parallel ergative constructions
