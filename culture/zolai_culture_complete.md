## 1. Zomi People History & Identity

### Who Are the Zomi?

**Zomi** (Zo + mi = "Zo people") are a Tibeto-Burman ethnic group whose ancestor is **Zo**. They primarily inhabit:

- **Chin State, Myanmar** (historical heartland — Zogam)
- **Manipur and Mizoram, India** (northeast India)
- **Southeastern Bangladesh**
- **Diaspora**: Tulsa, Oklahoma, USA (largest Zomi diaspora community)

### Key Vocabulary

| Zolai | English | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| `Zomi` | Zomi people | Zo + mi (people) |
| `Zogam` | Zo land | Zo + gam (land) |
| `Zo` | Ancestor | The original ancestor |
| `Khanggui` | Genealogy | Clan lineage records |
| `Beh` | Clan | Family/clan unit |
| `Phung` | Custom | Traditional way |

### Historical Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| Pre-history | Zomi live in Chindwin Valley plains, call themselves Zo |
| 862 AD | Tang dynasty records Zo kingdom in Chindwin Valley |
| 1800s | British colonization; "Chin" and "Kuki" labels imposed |
| 1946 | Lushai Hills people adopt "Mizo" (Zo people) identity |
| 1948 | **20 February** — Chins adopt democratic administration; later becomes Zomi National Day |
| 1953 | Baptist Associations of Tedim, Falam, Hakha adopt "Zomi" as national name |
| 1993 | **Zomi Reunification Organisation (ZRO)** formed at Phapian, Kachin State |

### Zomi Nam Ni — Zomi National Day

**20 February** is observed as Zomi National Day (Zomi Nam Ni). It marks the day in 1948 when the Chin people adopted a democratic system, dispensing with traditional chieftaincies.

### Zogam — The Zo Homeland

**Zogam** (Zo + gam = "Zo land") refers to the ancestral Zo homeland — a contiguous territory spanning:

- Chin State, Myanmar (core)
- Manipur hill districts, India
- Parts of Mizoram, India
- Chittagong Hill Tracts, Bangladesh

## 3. Traditional Customs

### Tengmaw — Marriage Custom

**Tengmaw** (lit. "bride price" / marriage arrangement) is the traditional Zomi marriage system.

#### Process

1. **Nau-in** — the young man's family sends a representative to the girl's family
2. **Tengmaw bawl** — negotiation of bride price between families
3. **Khua-in** — the wedding feast held at the bride's village
4. **Inn-lut** — the bride enters the groom's house

#### Bride Price Components

| Item | Zolai | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Cattle | `sa` | Traditional |
| Cloth | `puan` | Traditional |
| Rice beer | `zu` | Traditional |
| Money | `sum` | Modern addition |

### Minvoina — Naming Ceremony

**Minvoina** (lit. "name-giving") is the ceremony where a newborn receives their name, typically 3–7 days after birth.

#### Process

1. Family and community gather at the `inn` (house)
2. An elder (`Pu`) announces the chosen name
3. The name is often chosen based on:
   - Ancestor names (clan tradition)
   - Circumstances of birth (time, season, event)
   - Spiritual significance
4. `Zu` (rice beer) and food shared with guests
5. The child's name is recorded in the `khanggui` (genealogy)

#### Key Vocabulary

| `Minvoina` | Naming ceremony | Name-giving |

| `Khanggui` | Genealogy | Lineage record |
| `Naupang` | Child | Infant |
| `Pu` | Elder | Honorific |

### Hnat — Labor Exchange

**Hnat** is the traditional communal labor system where community members help each other with large tasks without payment — reciprocal labor exchange.

#### How It Works

- A family needing help calls a `hnat`
- Neighbors and clan members come to help for the day
- The host family provides food and `zu` (rice beer)
- The helpers expect the same help in return when they need it

## 4. Religious Practices

### Christianity

The majority of Zomi are **Baptist Christians**. Christianity arrived in the early 20th century through American Baptist missionaries.

#### Key Vocabulary

| `Topa` | Lord | Master |
| `Thumna` | Prayer | Communication with God |
| `Laisiangtho` | Bible | Holy book |
| `Biakna` | Worship | Religious service |
| `Khangsinna` | Salvation | Spiritual deliverance |

#### Church Organization

| Role | Zolai | Function |

| `Thumna upa` | Prayer leader | Intercession |
| `Sangna upa` | Teacher | Education |

### Traditional Beliefs

Before Christianity, the Zomi practiced **animism** and ancestor worship:

| Belief | Zolai | Description |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| `Kha` | Spirit | Spiritual beings |
| `Kha bawl` | Spirit offering | Ritual sacrifice |
| `Kha mu` | Spirit see | Divination |
| `Kha din` | Spirit place | Sacred site |
| `Thu upa` | Story elder | Oral tradition keeper |

### Syncretism

Many Zomi practices blend Christian and traditional elements:

- **Harvest thanksgiving** (Khuado) — combines Christian prayer with traditional celebration
- **Naming ceremonies** — Christian names with traditional rituals
- **Funeral rites** — Christian burial with traditional mourning practices

- **Communal meals** are central to festivals and gatherings

## 7. Traditional Clothing

### Puan — Traditional Cloth

**Puan** is the traditional handwoven cloth of the Zomi people, woven by women on backstrap looms.

#### Types of Puan

| Type | Zolai | Description | Occasion |
|------|-------|-------------|----------|
| `Puan tualpi` | Ceremonial shawl | Striped, colorful | Festivals, weddings |
| `Puan bansau` | Long cloth wrap | Daily wear | Traditional |
| `Puan siangtho` | White/pure cloth | Mourning | Burial |
| `Puan hoih` | Fine/beautiful cloth | Gifts | Bride price |

#### Weaving Vocabulary

| Zolai | English | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| `Puan` | Cloth/fabric | Traditional textile |
| `Puan gel` | Weave cloth | Weaving action |
| `Puan gelpa` | Male weaver | Weaver (male) |
| `Puan gelnu` | Female weaver | Weaver (female) |
| `Puan silh` | Wear cloth | Dressing |
| `Puan piak` | Give cloth | Gift-giving |
| `Tualpi` | Ceremonial shawl | Special garment |
| `Puan dan` | Cloth pattern | Design |

### Cultural Significance

- **Weaving skill** (`puan gel`) was a mark of a woman's worth and readiness for marriage
- **Patterns** (`puan dan`) encode clan identity — different `beh` (clans) have distinct patterns
- **Puan** is still worn at `Khuado` (harvest festival) and major ceremonies

### Modern Clothing

Today, Zomi people wear a mix of traditional and modern clothing:

| Occasion | Clothing |
|----------|----------|
| Daily | Western clothing |
| Festivals | Traditional `puan` |
| Church | Formal Western + `puan` accents |
| Weddings | Bride in `puan tualpi`, groom in suit |

## 10. Taboos & Etiquette

### Social Taboos

| Taboo | Zolai | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| `Kei thei` | Don't know | Pretending to know |
| `Thu gen kei` | Don't speak | Speaking ill of others |
| `An ne kei` | Don't eat | Refusing hospitality |
| `Tui in kei` | Don't drink | Refusing drink |

### Etiquette Rules

| Situation | Proper Behavior | Zolai |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| Greeting elders | Stand, bow slightly | `Hi Pu.` |
| Entering a house | Ask permission | `Hong lut dih.` |
| Receiving food | Accept with both hands | `Lungdam.` |
| Leaving | Say goodbye | `Va ka hi.` |
| Meeting someone | Greet warmly | `Na dam hiam?` |

### Gender Roles

| Role | Male | Female |
|------|------|--------|
| Weaving | Rare | Primary |
| Farming | Primary | Secondary |
| Cooking | Rare | Primary |
| Leadership | Traditional | Community |

### Age Respect

| Term | Zolai | Usage |
|------|-------|-------|
| `Pu` | Elder (male) | Respectful address |
| `Nu` | Elder (female) | Respectful address |
| `Upa` | Chief/elder | Leadership |
| `Naupang` | Child | Young person |

### Hospitality Rules

1. **Welcome guests** with food and drink
2. **Offer zu** (rice beer) to visitors
3. **Share food** at communal meals
4. **Respect elders** in conversation
5. **Avoid arguments** in public
