## 1. Imperative System Overview

Zolai imperatives range from direct commands to polite requests, depending on the particle used.

### Direct Commands (No Softener)

| Form | Person | Example | Translation |
|------|--------|---------|-------------|
| `V + in` | singular | `Pai in.` | Go! |
| `V + un` | plural | `Pai un.` | Go! (all of you) |
| `V + (bare)` | any | `Pai!` | Go! (most direct) |

### Softened Commands (With Particles)

| Form | Person | Example | Translation |
|------|--------|---------|-------------|
| `V + aw` | any | `Ngaih aw.` | Think! (soft) |
| `V + ni` | hortative | `Sem ni.` | Let it be done. |
| `V + un` | polite request | `Hong maisak un.` | Please note. |
| `V + hen la` | very polite | `Hong lo phei ziau hen la.` | Please let come down. |
| `V + dih` | polite prohibition | `Pai kei dih.` | Please don't go. |

## 2. Softening Particles

### "aw" — Soft Command / Vocative

**Function:** Softens a command into a gentle instruction or call.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Ngaih aw**, ka copyright hong hi in | **Think**, my copyright is coming | `ngaih` (think) + `aw` (softener) |
| 2 | Hai pana kingah vitamin kisam veh **aw** | Vitamins from there are needed, **hey** | `veh` (use) + `aw` (softener) |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows the verb directly
- Creates a gentle, encouraging tone
- Can also function as vocative (calling attention)
- Similar to English "hey," "please" (mild)

**Pattern:** `Verb + aw.`

---

### "ni" — Hortative / Let

**Function:** Creates hortative mood — "let it be done" or "let us."

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Gah zihziah ta ci **ni**. | Let it be taken and distributed. | `ci` (QUOT) + `ni` (hortative) |
| 2 | Thu lam tawnin sem **ni** | Let it be done in the manner of the word | `sem` (do) + `ni` (hortative) |
| 3 | Mangngilh kei **ni**. | Let us not forget. | `mangngilh` (forget) + `kei` (NEG) + `ni` (hortative) |
| 4 | Na tukik phot **ning**. | Let you just return. | `tukik` (return) + `phot` (only) + `ning` (hortative variant) |

**Usage notes:**
- Creates "let" constructions
- Can be singular or plural
- Often used in religious/moral contexts
- Similar to English "let," "may"

**Pattern:** `Verb + ni/ning.`

---

### "un" — Please / Polite Request

**Function:** Softens a command into a polite request. Very common in editorial prose.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Sepkhialh gelkhialh a om leh hong maisak **un** | If there are errors, please note it | `maisak` (note) + `un` (please) |
| 2 | "Guai, i sunday school naupangte in hai pana kingah vitamin kisam veh aw" ci-in ama thu tawh hong lo phei ziau hen la | "Hey, our Sunday school children need vitamins" saying, with his word, please let it come down | `hen la` (please) |

**Usage notes:**
- Most common polite particle
- Creates "please" constructions
- Used in both formal and informal contexts
- Similar to English "please"

**Pattern:** `Verb + un.`

---

### "hen la" — Please (Very Polite)

**Function:** Creates very polite requests. More formal than `un`.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | ci-in ama thu tawh hong lo phei ziau **hen la** | saying, with his word, please let it come down | `phei ziau` (come down) + `hen la` (please) |

**Usage notes:**
- Two-particle combination: `hen` + `la`
- More formal than `un`
- Used in respectful/deferential contexts
- Similar to English "please" (formal)

**Pattern:** `Verb + hen la.`

---

### "dih" — Polite Prohibition

**Function:** Softens a negative command into a polite prohibition.

**Examples from general grammar:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Pai kei **dih**. | Please don't go. | `pai` (go) + `kei` (NEG) + `dih` (polite) |
| 2 | Ne kei **dih**. | Please don't eat. | `ne` (eat) + `kei` (NEG) + `dih` (polite) |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows negation `kei`
- Creates polite prohibition
- Less harsh than `kei in` (don't!)
- Similar to English "please don't"

**Pattern:** `Verb + kei + dih.`

---

### "ing" — Imperative (Plural/Formal)

**Function:** Creates imperative mood, often for plural subjects or formal contexts.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English | Analysis |
|---|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Khawlkik **ing**. | Return! | `khawlkik` (return) + `ing` (imperative) |

**Usage notes:**
- More formal than bare imperative
- Can address plural subjects
- Used in editorial/writing contexts
- Similar to English imperative (formal)

**Pattern:** `Verb + ing.`

## 3. Patterns from Article

### Pattern 1: Verb + aw (Soft Command)

```
Ngaih aw, ka copyright hong hi in.
[Think] [PART] 1SG copyright PRFV-be-IMPF.
"Think, my copyright is coming."
```

**Analysis:**
- `ngaih` = think (verb stem)
- `aw` = softener particle
- Creates gentle imperative
- Opens the article with soft command

---

### Pattern 2: Verb + ni (Hortative)

```
Gah zihziah ta ci ni.
[take] [distribute] [PART] [QUOT] [LET]
"Let it be taken and distributed."
```

**Analysis:**
- `gah` = take
- `zihziah` = distribute
- `ta` = particle (completive?)
- `ci` = quotative
- `ni` = hortative (let)
- Creates "let" construction

---

### Pattern 3: Verb + un (Please)

```
Sepkhialh gelkhialh a om leh hong maisak un.
[error] [mistake] 3SG.AGR-exist-if PRFV note-PART
"If there are errors, please note it."
```

**Analysis:**
- `sepkhialh` = error
- `gelkhialh` = mistake
- `a om leh` = if exists
- `hong` = PRFV (perfective)
- `maisak` = note/record
- `un` = please
- Creates polite request

---

### Pattern 4: Verb + hen la (Very Polite)

```
ci-in ama thu tawh hong lo phei ziau hen la.
[QUOT-IMPF] 3SG-word-with PRFV-down-come-EMPH please-PART
"saying, with his word, please let it come down."
```

**Analysis:**
- `ci-in` = quotative-purposive
- `ama thu tawh` = with his word
- `hong` = PRFV
- `lo` = down (directional)
- `phei` = come
- `ziau` = emphatic
- `hen la` = please (very polite)
- Creates very polite request

---

### Pattern 5: Verb + kei + ni (Negative Hortative)

```
Mangngilh kei ni.
[forget] [NEG] [LET]
"Let us not forget."
```

**Analysis:**
- `mangngilh` = forget
- `kei` = negation
- `ni` = hortative (let)
- Creates negative hortative: "let us not"

---

### Pattern 6: Verb + phot + ning (Only + Hortative)

```
Na tukik phot ning.
[2SG] [return] [only] [LET]
"Let you just return."
```

**Analysis:**
- `na` = you (2SG)
- `tukik` = return
- `phot` = only/just
- `ning` = hortative variant
- Creates "just let" construction

## Prose Style Analysis

The copyright article uses **multiple softening levels** for moral persuasion:

1. **Opens soft**: `Ngaih aw` (Think!) — gentle invitation
2. **Escalates to hortative**: `Sem ni` (Let it be done) — moral imperative
3. **Uses polite requests**: `Hong maisak un` (Please note) — community respect
4. **Very polite**: `Hong lo phei ziau hen la` (Please let come down) — deference
5. **Negative hortative**: `Mangngilh kei ni` (Let us not forget) — moral warning
6. **Direct imperative**: `Khawlkik ing` (Return!) — final command

**Effect:** The escalating softness creates a moral gradient — from gentle invitation to firm command.

## Key Grammar Notes

### Particle Position

All softening particles follow the verb directly:

```
[Verb] + [Particle]
Ngaih + aw
Sem + ni
Maisak + un
```

### Negation + Particles

Negative commands use `kei` before the particle:

```
[Verb] + kei + [Particle]
Mangngilh + kei + ni = Let us not forget
Pai + kei + dih = Please don't go
```

### Compound Particles

Some particles combine for extra politeness:

```
hen + la = very polite
phot + ning = just let
```
