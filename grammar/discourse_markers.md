## 1. Additive Connectors

### "Tua banah" — Moreover / Furthermore

**Definition:** Adds additional information to strengthen the previous point. Used at sentence beginnings.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Tua banah**, a mi leh sum a om pong hang, siamna nei mi a om kei leh, i nasep pen kuitungin muibun tuan het lo thei lai hi. | **Moreover**, wherever there are people and money, if there is no wise person, our work cannot be completely managed. |
| 2 | **Tua banah**, i theih limlim dingin Lai Siangtho buppi ei pau ngiat tawh i neih theih nadingin sum leh pai tampi banah... | **Moreover**, for us to know carefully, the entire Bible, with our mouth alone, to be able to have... |

**Usage notes:**
- Always sentence-initial
- Followed by a comma in written form
- Bridges two related but distinct points
- Similar to English "moreover," "furthermore," "in addition"

**Pattern:** `Tua banah, [additional point].`

---

### "Lem" — Also / Even

**Definition:** Adds a parallel or surprising additional element. Can be used mid-sentence or sentence-initially.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Lem** a kisa lo, leh dik a kisa lo pen, inntekte theihpihna lo tawh vai a kihawm ziau pen hi a... | **Also** it hasn't finished, and rightness hasn't finished yet, without people's knowledge, the matter moves by itself... |
| 2 | **Lem** a kisa lo | **Also** it hasn't finished |
| 3 | Haina leh kiphatsakna a kithuah lungsim hi ta a, VAISAH cihin zong gen khin zo lo hi. (parallel with "zong") | ...shame and offense, even "disaster" cannot be fully spoken of. |

**Usage notes:**
- Mid-sentence position most common
- Can introduce a surprising or unexpected addition
- Often paired with `zong` (also) for double emphasis
- Similar to English "also," "even," "too"

**Pattern:** `[Element] lem [additional element].`

---

### "Leh" — And / Also

**Definition:** Coordinating conjunction connecting nouns, phrases, or clauses.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | inntek hihna tawh hai pen naupangte piak ding a ki-it hi masa lo, i vok a kibah ding a phal lo bikbek cihna **leh** dik a kisa lo pen... | ...the thing to give children hasn't been thought of yet, and rightness hasn't finished yet... |
| 2 | "itna" **leh** "hehpihna" tawh kidim Pasian hi a... | known with "love" and "joy," is God... |
| 3 | "thuman" **leh** "thutak" a lenkip Pasian zong hi... | "truth" and "righteousness" accompany God also... |
| 4 | kibawlphat nadinga i zatsiam **leh** ei a hampha hi zaw cih pen... | and if we try to reconcile, that it is more important for us... |

**Usage notes:**
- Most versatile connector in Zolai
- Connects nouns: `itna leh hehpihna` (love and joy)
- Connects clauses: `i zatsiam leh ei a hampha` (if we try and it is important)
- Can function as conditional: `Na hoih i sep leh zong` (If you do good also)

**Pattern:** `[A] leh [B].`

---

### "Pian leh" — As well as / Becoming and

**Definition:** Connects a preceding state or action to a following consequence or parallel action.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Lungsim sau pai **pian leh** bang, "Upa pa in hong zahtak kei si e..." | If the heart becomes after the thought, "The elder has not permitted..." |
| 2 | Na hoih khat peuhpeuh i sep ding ciang, nawngkaina tuamtuam om thei. Pilvan nading zong tampi om. | When we each do good, various obstacles can exist. There is much to climb. (preceding context for `pian leh`) |

**Usage notes:**
- More specific than `leh` alone
- Implies a process of becoming or developing
- Often introduces a consequence or result
- Similar to English "as a result," "and so"

**Pattern:** `[State/Action] pian leh [Consequence/Parallel].`

## 2. Causal/Logical Connectors

### "Tua manin" — Therefore / That's why

**Definition:** Introduces a conclusion or result based on previously stated reasoning.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Tua manin** zahtakhuai ka sa semsem hi. | **Therefore**, I continuously take permission. |
| 2 | Ahihmanin vaipuak a na la i makaite pen zahtakna i piak siam ding pen thupi hi. (parallel concept) | Therefore the offering that was taken, the permission we must give is important. |

**Usage notes:**
- Sentence-initial position
- Followed by a comma
- Introduces logical conclusion
- Stronger than `ahihmanin` (therefore)
- Similar to English "therefore," "that is why," "consequently"

**Pattern:** `Tua manin, [conclusion].`

---

### "Tua hi a" — That is why

**Definition:** Similar to `tua manin` but emphasizes the causal link more strongly.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Tua hi a**, i Tedim Lai Siangtho pen inntek vaipuak hong na laksak pen a cil pek panin BSM leh ZCLS hi a... | **That is why**, our Tedim Bible, the offering of sincerity that was taken, from which it was said, is BSM and ZCLS... |

**Usage notes:**
- Sentence-initial
- Stronger causal emphasis than `tua manin`
- Implies "for this very reason"
- Similar to English "that is why," "for this reason"

**Pattern:** `Tua hi a, [reason-based conclusion].`

---

### "Ahihmanin" — Therefore / So

**Definition:** Introduces a conclusion based on the preceding statement.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | ...ahihmanin vaipuak a na la i makaite pen zahtakna i piak siam ding pen thupi hi. | ...therefore the offering that was taken and given to us, the permission we must give is important. |

**Usage notes:**
- Sentence-medial or initial
- Less emphatic than `tua manin`
- Can be used in subordinate clauses
- Similar to English "therefore," "so," "consequently"

**Pattern:** `[Reason], ahihmanin [conclusion].`

## 3. Contrastive Connectors

### "Ahizongin" — However / Nevertheless

**Definition:** Introduces a contrast or concession to the previous point.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Ahizongin** a thu paizia thei gegu napi a, "Phalna la nuam lo" "Inntekte thuzak masak ding kisam sa lo" cihte pawl bel... | **However**, a person who understands the meaning of the word, "Doesn't want to take permission" "Doesn't need people to believe first"... |
| 2 | **Ahizongin** a thu paizia i tel kul hi. | **However**, we must know the meaning of the word. |

**Usage notes:**
- Sentence-initial
- Always followed by a comma
- Introduces contrasting or unexpected information
- Stronger contrast than `ahih leh`
- Similar to English "however," "nevertheless," "on the other hand"

**Pattern:** `Ahizongin, [contrasting point].`

---

### "Tua leh" — So / Then

**Definition:** Introduces a consequence or next step in reasoning.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Tua leh**, Tedim Lai Siangtho pen BSM leh ZCLS-te neihsa maw? | **So**, the Tedim Bible, is it BSM and ZCLS's own? |

**Usage notes:**
- Sentence-initial
- Bridges reasoning to a new question or conclusion
- Less emphatic than `tua manin`
- Similar to English "so," "then," "well"

**Pattern:** `Tua leh, [next point/question].`

## 4. Temporal Connectors

### "Ci-in" — Having said / In the manner of

**Definition:** Quotative or purposive connector linking reported speech to action.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | "Guai, i sunday school naupangte in hai pana kingah vitamin kisam veh aw" **ci-in** ama thu tawh hong lo phei ziau hen la... | "Hey, our Sunday school children need vitamins from there" **saying**, with his word, please let it come down... |
| 2 | Pasian nasepna **ci-in** na hawm keei mawk leh... | **for** God's work, you print it fully... |
| 3 | "Khawlkik ing. Sepkhialh gelkhialh a om leh hong maisak un" **ci-a** hong thumkik ngiat... | "Return. If there are errors, please note it" **saying**, emphasized indeed... |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows quoted speech
- Can be purposive (`ci-in` = "in order to")
- Can be quotative (`ci-a` = "saying")
- Similar to English "saying," "in order to," "that"

**Pattern:** `"[Quote]" ci-in/ ci-a [action].`

---

### "Ciang" — When / At the time of

**Definition:** Temporal connector marking when an action occurs.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Na hoih khat peuhpeuh i sep **ding** ciang, nawngkaina tuamtuam om thei. | When we each do good, various obstacles can exist. |
| 2 | A kik **ciangin** lampi man tawnin gelkik ding ci-in ka na hoihlak hi. | When it returns, saying it will move back in full manner, I desire it for you. |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows a verb or verb phrase
- Can be `ciang` (when) or `ciangin` (when-IMPF)
- Similar to English "when," "at the time that"

**Pattern:** `[Action] ciang(in), [subsequent event].`

## 5. Emphasis Particles

### "Pen" — Focus/Topic Marker

**Definition:** Marks the focus or topic of a sentence, emphasizing the preceding element.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Tua **pen** khatvei bek hi lo ding. | That is only the first time. |
| 2 | A thu paizia **pen**, inntek hihna tawh... | The meaning of the word is, with sincerity... |
| 3 | I Tedim Lai Siangtho **pen**... | Our Tedim Bible is... |
| 4 | A "thu" **pen** a thupi hi zaw hi. | Its "word" is more important indeed. |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows the focused element
- Can be used sentence-initially or mid-sentence
- Creates cleft-like emphasis
- Similar to English "it is... that," "the thing that"

**Pattern:** `[Focused element] pen [predicate].`

---

### "Mahmah" — Very / Emphatic

**Definition:** Intensifier adding emphasis to the preceding element.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Na it **mahmah** na vok | You love your animal **very much** |
| 2 | Tua hi a, i Tedim Lai Siangtho pen inntek vaipuak hong na laksak pen a cil pek panin BSM leh ZCLS hi a, i zahtak **mahmah** dingte hi. | ...they are the ones we must **truly** permit. |
| 3 | Limtak i thudot masak dingte hi. | **Truly**, they are the ones we must first believe. |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows the element being intensified
- Can intensify verbs, adjectives, or entire phrases
- Similar to English "very," "truly," "indeed"

**Pattern:** `[Element] mahmah [predicate].`

---

### "Bel" — Indeed / Truly

**Definition:** Emphatic particle confirming or intensifying the statement.

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Tua pen khatvei **bek** hi lo ding. | That is **only** the first time. |
| 2 | cih-te pawl **bel**, haina leh kiphatsakna a kithuah lungsim hi ta a... | things that are **indeed**, a heart that creates shame and offense yet... |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows the element being emphasized
- Stronger than `pen` alone
- Similar to English "indeed," "truly," "certainly"

**Pattern:** `[Element] bel [predicate].`

---

### "Zaw" — More / Even more

**Definition:** Comparative emphasis, indicating "more than" or "even more."

**Examples from article:**

| # | Zolai | English |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | A "thu" pen a thupi hi **zaw** hi. | Its "word" is **more** important indeed. |
| 2 | cihna hi **zaw** a, thu hoih thu man deihna ahi hi. | That is what it is **indeed**, it is the desire for the good and true word. |

**Usage notes:**
- Follows the adjective or predicate
- Creates comparative emphasis
- Similar to English "more," "even more," "indeed"

**Pattern:** `[Adjective] hi zaw hi.`

## Prose Style Notes

The copyright article uses **formal/editorial prose** with:

1. **Discourse chain**: `Tua leh` → `Tua banah` → `Tua manin` → `Tua hi a` creates logical flow
2. **Rhetorical questions**: `Na lung a kim diam?` repeated for emphasis
3. **Ethical framing**: `inntek` (sincerity) repeated 8+ times as core concept
4. **Contrastive structure**: `Ahizongin` introduces counterpoints
5. **Causal reasoning**: `Tua manin` and `Tua hi a` draw conclusions
6. **Loan word integration**: `copyright`, `vitamin`, `quality` used naturally
