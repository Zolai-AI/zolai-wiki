# Zolai Register Analysis — Complete Guide
> Last updated: 2026-09-10

> How formality, context, and relationship shape language choice in Tedim Zolai.
> Sources: ZVS 2018, Zolai Khanggui, Zolai Sinna Bu, zomidaily corpus, Bible corpus, article analyses, worship songs.

---

## Overview

Tedim Zolai has **five distinct social registers**. Choosing the wrong register is one of the most common errors — using church language in casual conversation sounds stiff; using casual speech in prayer is disrespectful; using formal language in a song is unnatural.

| Register | Zolai Name | Context | Key Markers |
|----------|------------|---------|-------------|
| **Formal/Biblical** | Laisiangtho pau | Church, prayer, scripture, sermons | Full pronouns, `hi hi`, archaic vocab, ergative `in` always explicit |
| **Written/Literary** | Zolai gelh | News, books, official letters, wiki | ZVS standard, full morphology, `ci hi`/`ci-in` quotation |
| **Semi-formal** | Thugen dan | School, meetings, speeches, greetings | Standard grammar, no contractions, honorific titles |
| **Conversational** | Pau dan | Daily life, family, friends, markets | Pronoun drop, contractions, `maw`/`hia`/`diam` questions |
| **Intimate/Poetic** | Lapau / Zola | Songs, poetry, lullabies, proverbs | Archaic forms, metaphor, reduplication, vocative `aw` |

---

## 1. Formal/Biblical Register (Laisiangtho Pau)

**Used in:** Church services, prayer, scripture reading, sermons, funerals, weddings, religious writing.

### 1.1 Key Features

| Feature | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Subject pronoun | Always stated | `Kei in...` (never dropped) |
| Emphatic declarative | `hi hi` | `A hoih hi hi.` — It is truly good. |
| Copula | `ahi hi` | `Amah Topa ahi hi.` — He is the Lord. |
| Ergative `in` | Always explicit | `Pasian in leitung a piangsak hi.` |
| Jussive | `hen` | `Om ta hen.` — Let there be. |
| Archaic vocabulary | `Topa`, `Pasian`, `Khrih` | Lord, God, Christ |
| Narrative connectors | `Tua ciangin`, `A kipat cilin` | After that, In the beginning |

### 1.2 Example

```
Pasian in leitung mite hong it mahmah ahih manin,
amah a Tapa a um mi khempeuh kisia loin
nuntak tawntungna a ngahna dingun
khat bek a neih a Tapa a pia hi.
— John 3:16 (Parallel Corpus)
```

**Translation:** Because God loved the people of the earth so much, he gave his only Son to all who believe in him, so that they may have eternal life.

### 1.3 Vocabulary

| Concept | Formal/Biblical | Modern/Secular |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Therefore | `Tua ahih ciangin` | `Tua ahih manin` |
| Because | `Bang hang hiam cih leh` | `Ahih manin` |
| Lord | `Topa` | `Uliante` (officials/bosses) |
| God | `Pasian` | `Pasian` (universal) |
| Salvation | `Hotkhiatna` | — |
| Blessing | `Thupha` | — |
| Holy | `Siangtho` | — |

### 1.4 Grammar Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Function |
|---------|---------|----------|
| `... ci hi` | `"Ka pai ding" ci hi.` | Direct speech quotation |
| `ci-in` | `... ci-in genkhia hi.` | Quotative connective |
| `... hi hi` | `A hoih hi hi.` | Emphatic declarative |
| `... hen` | `Om ta hen.` | Jussive (let/may) |
| `... ahi hi` | `Amah Pasian ahi hi.` | Copula identification |

## 2. Written/Literary Register (Zolai Gelh)

**Used in:** Newspapers (Zomidaily), books, official letters, wiki, formal documents, academic writing.

### 2.1 Key Features

| Feature | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| ZVS 2018 orthography | Strictly followed | All spelling rules enforced |
| `noun khat word khat` | One concept = one word | No spaces within compounds |
| Full verb morphology | No contractions | All affixes explicit |
| Quotation | `ci hi` / `ci-in` | `ci hi` for reported speech |
| No pronoun drop | All subjects stated | Full S+O+V structure |
| Date format | `[year]' kum [month] kha [day] ni` | `2026' kum April kha 14 ni` |

### 2.2 Example

```
Zomi mite in amau' pau leh ngeina tawntung theihna dingin
nasem tampi a bawl-te hi.
— Zomidaily style
```

**Translation:** The Zomi people have done many works to preserve their language and customs.

### 2.3 News Register Patterns (from zomidaily, 511K sentences)

| Pattern | Frequency | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ergative `in` | 189,358 (37%) | `pasian in vantung a piangsak hi.` |
| Future `ding` | 97,787 (19.1%) | `a pai ding hi.` |
| Content question `bang` | 27,567 (5.4%) | `bang hang pai na hiam?` |
| Quotative `ci` | 24,753 (4.8%) | `"... ci hi.` |
| Progressive `lai` | 20,878 (4.1%) | `a ne lai hi.` |
| Past `ta` | 15,548 (3%) | `ka pai ta hi.` |

### 2.4 Key Differences from Bible Register

| Feature | Bible | News/Written |
|---------|-------|--------------|
| Future tense | 32% | 19.1% |
| Content questions | 3% | 5.4% |
| Negation `kei` | Rare | 4.3% |
| Discourse markers | Few | Many (`mahmah`, `uh`, `ahihi`) |
| Loan words | Minimal | Extensive (Malaysia, COVID) |

## 3. Semi-Formal Register

**Used in:** School, community meetings, public speeches, formal greetings, interviews.

### 3.1 Key Features

| Feature | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Standard grammar | Slightly relaxed | Standard but less rigid than written |
| Subject pronouns | Usually stated | `Kei in...` (usually present) |
| Declarative | `hi` (not `hi hi`) | `Ka pai hi.` |
| Honorific titles | `Pu`, `Pi`, `U` | `Pu Thang aw,` |
| Direct address | `aw` vocative | `Pu Thang aw, na pai hiam?` |

### 3.2 Example

```
Pu Thang aw, tu ni-in i kikhawm ahih manin lungdam mahmah hi.
(Respected Thang, I am very glad we gathered today.)
```

### 3.3 Honorific System

| Title | Use | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| `Pu` | Elder/respected male | `Pu Thang` |
| `Pi` | Elder/respected female | `Pi Hlawn` |
| `U` | Younger male (respectful) | `U Kham` |
| `Topa` | Religious leader | `Topa Cope` |
| `Rev.` | Reverend | `Rev. Khup Za Go` |

### 3.4 Vocabulary

| Concept | Semi-formal | Formal/Biblical | Casual |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| Meeting | `tonzang` | `tonkhawmna` | `ton` |
| Agreement | `hoihtak` | `hoihtak` | `hoih` |
| Work | `nasem` | `sepna` | `sem` |
| News | `thupi` | `thu` | `thu` |

## 4. Conversational Register (Pau Dan)

**Used in:** Family, friends, markets, casual daily life, text messages, phone calls.

### 4.1 Key Features

| Feature | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Subject pronoun drop | When agreement marker clear | `An ka ne hi.` (`kei` dropped) |
| Contracted forms | `na'ng` = you | `Dam na hi hiam?` |
| Tag question | `maw` | `Hoih maw?` — Good, right? |
| Soft question | `hia` | `Na dam hia?` — You okay? |
| Gentle question | `diam` | `Na pai ding diam?` — Will you go? |
| Shorter sentences | Less explicit marking | Minimal particles |

### 4.2 Example Dialogue

```
A: Dam na hi hiam?        (Are you well?)
B: Dam ka hi. Na zong dam hiam?  (I'm well. Are you well too?)
A: Ka dam. An na ne zo hiam?     (I'm well. Have you eaten?)
B: Ne zo khin hi. Lungdam.       (I have eaten. Thanks.)
```

### 4.3 Question Markers Comparison

| Marker | Register | Function | Example |
|--------|----------|----------|---------|
| `hiam` | Formal/semi-formal | Yes/no question | `Na pai hiam?` |
| `hia` | Conversational | Soft yes/no question | `Na dam hia?` |
| `diam` | Conversational | Gentle future question | `Na pai ding diam?` |
| `maw` | Conversational | Tag question | `Hoih maw?` |
| `bang` | All registers | Content question | `Bang na sem hiam?` |

### 4.4 Pronoun Dropping Rules

| Full Form | Conversational Form | Rule |
|-----------|---------------------|------|
| `Kei ka pai hi.` | `Ka pai hi.` | `Kei` dropped (agreement `ka` present) |
| `Kei ka pai ve.` | `Pai ing.` | Both pronoun and agreement dropped |
| `Kei ka ne hi.` | `An ka ne hi.` | Past tense retained, pronoun dropped |
| `Na pai hiam?` | `Pai ing?` | Future/context clear |

### 4.5 Declarative Marker: `hi` vs `ve`

| Register | Pattern | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Formal | `[Verb] + hi.` | `Ka pai hi.` — I go. |
| Informal | `[Verb] + ve.` | `Ka pai ve.` — I'm going. (casual) |
| Mixed | `[Verb] + hi ve.` | `Ka pai hi ve.` — Softening the fact. |

### 4.6 Common Conversational Expressions

| Expression | Meaning | Context |
|------------|---------|---------|
| `Lungdam ka hi.` | I'm happy/well. | Greeting response |
| `Dam na hi hiam?` | Are you well? | Greeting |
| `Ne zo khin hi.` | I have eaten. | After meal |
| `Lungdam mahmah hi.` | Very happy/grateful. | Expression of thanks |
| `Hoih maw?` | Good, right? | Tag question |
| `Na thei hiam?` | Do you know? | Inquiry |

## 5. Intimate/Poetic Register (Lapau / Zola)

**Used in:** Traditional songs (Zola), poetry (Pasaltit), lullabies, proverbs, children's songs.

### 5.1 Key Features

| Feature | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Archaic vocabulary | Preserved forms | Old Zolai words |
| Reduplication | For rhythm | `ciakciak`, `vingveng`, `mahmah` |
| Parallelism | Two lines, same structure | Balanced phrasing |
| Metaphor | `lung` compounds freely | Rich imagery |
| Vocative `aw` | Direct address | `Zomi aw!`, `Pasian aw!` |
| Shortened forms | For meter | Truncated words |

### 5.2 Example

```
Khapi aw hong kia, hong kia
Na koi lai na tat, na tat
(Come little bird, come
Wherever you go, go)
— Zolai Sinna Bu (children's song)
```

### 5.3 Poetic Devices

| Device | Pattern | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Parallelism | Line 1 ≈ Line 2 (different words) | `Khapi aw hong kia` / `Na koi lai na tat` |
| Reduplication | AABB or ABAB | `dangdang`, `memmam`, `lemlem` |
| Vocative | `aw` at end of noun | `Zomi aw!`, `Pasian aw!` |
| Metaphor | `lung` compounds | `lungdam`, `lungtang`, `lungngai` |
| Archaic forms | Old vocabulary | Words not used in modern speech |

## 7. Register Switching

### 7.1 How Speakers Switch Registers

Zolai speakers fluidly switch registers depending on audience, topic, and emotional state.

| Scenario | Register Shift | Example |
|----------|----------------|---------|
| Preacher → congregation | Biblical → Semi-formal | `Pasian in...` → `I kikhawm hi...` |
| News anchor → family | Written → Conversational | `Zomite te in...` → `Na ne zo hiam?` |
| Teacher → students | Semi-formal → Conversational | `Pu Thang aw...` → `Na thei hiam?` |
| Song writer → audience | Poetic → All | Archaic forms + metaphor |
| Formal letter → phone call | Written → Conversational | Full morphology → contractions |

### 7.2 Common Register Errors

| Mistake | Wrong | Correct |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Church language in casual speech | `Kei in an ka ne hi hi.` | `An ka ne hi.` |
| Casual speech in prayer | `Pasian, dam ka hi maw?` | `Pasian aw, ka dam hi.` |
| Wrong honorific (to elder) | `Thang, pai in.` | `Pu Thang aw, pai ding na hi hiam?` |
| Dropping `in` in formal writing | `Pasian leitung piangsak hi.` | `Pasian in leitung a piangsak hi.` |
| Using `lo` with `a` agreement | `A pai lo hi.` | `Pai lo hi.` |
| Wrong question word order | `Bang hang na pai hiam?` | `Bang hang pai na hiam?` |

### 7.3 Register-Appropriate Greetings

| Situation | Register | Greeting | Response |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Meeting elder | Semi-formal | `Pu Thang aw, lungdam na hi hiam?` | `Lungdam ka hi.` |
| Friends | Conversational | `Dam na hi hiam?` | `Dam ka hi.` |
| Church | Biblical | `Laisiangtho sunga lungdam...` | `Lungdam ka hi.` |
| Phone call | Conversational | `Dam na hi hiam?` | `Dam ka hi. Na zong dam hiam?` |
| Formal letter | Written | `Pu Thang aw, na lungdam hi hiam?` | `Lungdam ka hi.` |

## 9. AI Tutor Implementation

### Register Training by Level

| Level | Register Focus | Teaching Goal |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| A1/A2 | Conversational | Teach `hi` and `hiam` exclusively |
| B1/B2 | Semi-formal | Introduce `ve` and `hia` for naturalness |
| C1/C2 | All registers | Challenge to switch between registers |

### Example Exercises

**A1:** Translate "I go." → `Ka pai hi.`
**A2:** Translate "Are you going?" → `Na pai hiam?`
**B1:** Translate "I'm going." (casual) → `Ka pai ve.`
**B2:** Translate "As for me, I will go." → `Kei pen ka pai ding hi.`
**C1:** Rewrite formal sentence as casual: `Kei in an ka ne hi hi.` → `An ka ne hi.`
**C2:** Tell a story in formal speech, then retell it to a friend conversationally.
