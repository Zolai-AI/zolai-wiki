# Zolai Grammar Patterns from Online Resources

## Overview
This document contains grammar patterns extracted from online resources including:
- Paumkim Zomi Guide (particles, word structure)
- Zolai Grammar Vol 1 (book)
- Zolai Sinna (learning textbook)
- Grammar Essentials (website)

## 1. Particle System (from Paumkim)

### Prefixes (merge RIGHT → to the following word)
| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| ka- | I, my (1st person singular) | ka paai → I go |
| na- | you, your (2nd person singular) | na paai → you go |
| a- | he/she, his/her (3rd person) | a paai → he/she goes |
| i- / ih- | we, our (1st person plural) | i paai → we go |
| ki- | reflexive, middle voice | ki paai → go oneself |

**Note:** ka-, na-, a-, i- are usually written separate from the verb. ki- is always merged.

### Suffixes (merge LEFT ← to the preceding word)
| Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| -te | plural marker | mi + te → mite |
| -ding | future / will | paai + ding → paaiding |
| -ta | already / completed | om + ta → omta |
| -ah | in / at / on (locative) | khawm + ah → khawmah |
| -in | by / with (instrumental) | thei + in → theiin |
| -'n | subject marker (agentive) | Zeisu + 'n → Zeisu'n |
| -pa | father / male | Topa from To + pa |
| -pi | big / large / mother | tuipi from tui + pi |
| -cing | completeness | kicing from ki + cing |
| -gah | berry / hanging fruit | singgah from sing + gah |
| -la | song | Zola from Zo + la |

## 2. Verb Patterns

### ki- (Reflexive/Middle Voice)
| Base | With ki- | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| khawm | kikhawm | gather oneself |
| cing | kicing | be filled |
| sim | kisim | be read |
| neih | kineih | be had / exist |
| paai | kipaai | go oneself / depart |

### Chaining Effect
Particles stack naturally:
1. ki + khawm → kikhawm (oneself + gather)
2. kikhawm + ding → kikhawmding (will gather)
3. kikhawmding + in → kikhawmdingin (by gathering)

Example:
- mi + te → mite (people)
- mite + 'n → mite'n (people - subject)
- mite'n + hong it → mite'n hong it (people love us)
- Mite'n hong it hi. → "People love us."

## 3. Reduplication
When a word repeats for emphasis or duration:
| Base | Reduplicated | Meaning |
|------|--------------|---------|
| sem | semsem | doing and doing (continuous) |
| pai | paipai | going on and on |
| tawm | tawmtawm | a little bit |
| mahn | mahmah | very much |

## 4. Apostrophe Usage
Apostrophe indicates contraction or possessive:
- Zeisu'n → Zeisu (subject)
- David'pa → David's father
- David'ta → David's son
- Egypt-ah → in Egypt

## 5. Tense Markers
From Bible patterns:
- **ding-hi**: 6,854 occurrences (future)
- **S-in-O-uh-hi**: 4,394 occurrences (3rd person plural)
- **ciangin-a**: 787 occurrences (temporal)
- **a-lo**: 164 occurrences (negation)

## 6. Sentence Structure
SOV (Subject-Object-Verb):
- Pasian leitung bawl hi. (God created the world)
- Ka ne hi. (I eat)
- Na pai hi. (You go)

## 7. Negation Patterns
- **1st/2nd person**: Use "kei" (NOT "lo")
  - Ka pai kei hi. (I don't go)
- **3rd person**: Use "lo" or "kei"
  - Pai lo hi. (He didn't go)
- **Future negation**: Use "lo ding"
  - Kpai lo ding hi. (I will not go)

## 8. Question Patterns
- **Yes/no questions**: Use "hiam"
  - Na pai hiam? (Did you go?)
- **Future questions**: Use "diam"
  - Na pai diam? (Will you go?)
- **Content questions**: Use "bang hang"
  - Bang hang na pai hiam? (Why do you go?)

## 9. Pronoun Agreement

### Agreement Markers (pre-verbal, always present)
These go before the verb and mark the person of the subject:
- **ka** = 1st person singular agreement marker (I)
- **na** = 2nd person singular agreement marker (you)
- **a** = 3rd person singular agreement marker (he/she/it)
- **i** = 1st person plural agreement marker (we)
- **uh** = plural marker (NOT "they" pronoun). 21,992 occurrences.

### Standalone Pronouns (used for emphasis or explicit reference)
These are used when you want to emphasize the subject or make it explicit:
- **kei** = I (emphatic pronoun)
- **nang** = you (emphatic pronoun)
- **amah** = he/she/it (standalone pronoun, used for emphasis)
- **amaute** = they (emphatic pronoun)
- **naute** = you all (emphatic pronoun)

### Key Difference: "a" vs "amah"
- **"a"** = agreement marker (goes before verb): "a pai hi" = he goes
- **"amah"** = standalone pronoun (used for emphasis): "amah a pai hi" = HE goes
- Both are correct, but "amah" emphasizes the subject

## 10. Verb Conjugation
- **Base**: ne (eat)
- **With subject**: ka ne, na ne, a ne
- **Future**: ne ding, ne ding hi
- **Negation**: ne kei, ne lo, ne lo ding
- **Question**: ne hiam, ne diam
- **Continuous**: ne ngei, ne ngai
