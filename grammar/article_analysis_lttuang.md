### Interview Part-1 (Moses on leading Israelites):

Kei: Pu Mo aw dam maw
Moses: Pasian Hehpihna tawh dam mah ing ei, nang ae dam maw
Kei: Kei zongh Pasian Hehpihna tawh dam mah ing ei
Kei: Egypt gam pan paikhiat lai aa makaipi hi napi, Canaan gam na lut khak loh vetse pen bang danin na feel aa le?
Moses: Hih dotna mawk bel, Canaan gam ka gal-et lai bang hong phawk sak cei.
Pasian in Canaan gam lut kha lo ding hong cih ciang bel, heh peuhmah kei ing. Ka lungsim khat si duai in heh zawh nading tha zongh a nei zo nawn lo ziau hi mai ing.
"Canaan gam ut lua, Canaan gam ii nopsakna teng lunggulh lua in lut kha lo" cih dan sangin "Pasian Thu ka man loh man hizen aa maw" cih dan hangin ka lungsim a si hizaw veh aw. Pasian in biakpiakna sang' thumanna deihzaw ahi aw, Tuang.
Hong gen suak lai vet leng, Canaan gam zongh a ut lo maw ci leng, a ut lua ae, tua hang mah aa zongh, mualdawn ah kah kaikai aa a gal-et liang hiveng. 
Ahih hang, ka phawk khak thu khat ah, "khat veivei ciang, ei pen a lamh ding i hi aa, mi dangte pen a lut ding, ei pen a suan ding, mi dangte pen a gah lo ding cih bang na hi thei hi."
Kei: Pasian a muang zo lo teng Canaan gam lut kha lo ci leng, Pasian a muang Joshua leh Caleb bel lut kha-in nang na lut khak loh pen, Pasian na muan loh man a kici thei dia?
Moses: Pasian bel muang ing in, ahih hang Canaan gam pen mipite hangin lut khak loh ding mah ka singim det thu khat hi. Pasian in Canaan gam hong tunpih zo lo ding aa, a muang lo cih bang hiloin, kei leh mipite hangin Pasian in hong tunpih nop loh khak ding a lunghimawh cih dan hizaw ing.
En ve, sawlbawk kuang tawh hong sap lai zongh, Pasian a muang lo hilo, kei leh kei a kimuang lo mah ae...
Tua khit ciang, mipite in milim khawng na bia uh, na phun sesu uh, makaite hong hawmthawh cih bang dan ka muh teh, Pasian a lungkim mawk mah diam ci aa, kei nangawn mipite tangin Hell ah hong tung sak in a na ci man liang hiveng...
Tua hi-in, Pasian liatna, ama sep theihna mah mitsuan tinten ding kimlai, ei hih zawh loh ding ei dinmun khawng i etzawk khak leh, muanlahna hong lut thei hi ei... Kei bang kei leh kei ka ki-et ciang, mipite ka muh ciang, mailam ding ka ngaihsut ciang lungnop nading mahmah om lopi, mate na ci na... Pasian mah en tiuteu ni ei.
Kei: Hi mah ei, tua nong gente thupi thei mahmah ei. OK, a hoihzaw deuh lam kidong ni ae... Canaan gam lut lohna, Pasian muan lohna cihte sang' hehee.... ci leng - na khan sungah nop na sakpen hun bang hun hi ding ahia le?
Moses: Part 2 ah na sim sak ve...

### Interview Part-2 (Moses on happiness):
[Same interviewer question as end of Part 1]
Moses: Oh... santuipi ka kaanna uh mah nopsapen dingin nong um hia aih kei leh, suangpi pan tui a phul ziahziah laitak mah noppen dingin na um hia? Egypt kumpi innpi sungah ka om hilhial lai ae, nopzaw dingin na um na? Na lamdang khawng lak ziahziah, mipite mai ah thugen ziahziah, mipite makaih ziahziah cih dan khawng mah a noppen hi dingin na um hia?
Tuate zongh nop mah, nop lo ci kei ing. Ahih hang' nop ka sak penpen hun bel, mi mai ah zongh hise lo, kuaman hong theihpih loh hang' Pasian tawh ka kiho laitak hong ompihna mahmah nopsapen ngaungau ing. Sinai mual tungah ae kei leh Pasian bek lian hiveng, a nop dan, mite khutbetna cih zongh om lo, mi dangte aw khawng kiza lo, Pasian aw bek lian hi ziau. 
Hih mun ah maw, Pasian in ae, ama vangliatna bek hong lak hilo, nuntak khuasak kalsuan zia ding zongh hong hilh hi lai hi. Biakbiak ding, phatphat laamlaam ding bek hilo, hong thupiakte zuih ding, nuntakpih ding zongh hong phawk sak nuam ing ei...
Tun no hun ah pen maw Tuang, mite in attention ngah nuam mahmah uh hi. Mite in a kimukhia hauhna, lawhcinna bek en uh hi. Tua te bek tawh a thupi leh thupi lo khen tamawk uh hi. Tua bangte in hun nuam penpen ka cih hih kong gen dante phawk kha lo, ngah kha lo thei uh hi.
Kei: Kalaisai, hi lua peuhmah ei. Lungdam taktak ei. Tua leh, haksa na sak penpen hun ae leh, nong gen thei lai dia?

### Interview Part-3 (Moses on suffering):
Kei: Kalaisai, hi lua peuhmah ei. Lungdam taktak ei. Tua leh, haksa na sak penpen hun khat, nong gen thei lai dia?
Moses: Haksat hun pen khatvei bek cih bang hi kei. 
- Sehnel gam bangah tui om lo, an om lo, nektawm ding haksa, tua kawmkal ah mipite in hong phunsan uh cih bang hi zel mai. Ahih hang' tua teng khempeuh Pasian in hong zawhpih hi.
Kei bang Pasian tungah "Bang hangin hih mi teng ii vangik kei tungah hong suan na hiam?" "Amau teng kei suaksak ka sung piangte a hiam?" "Kei bek in ka pua zo kei hi, kei adingin gik lua hi." bang ka ci liang hi.
Haksa ka sak penpen hun bel, Pharoah mah ah ka dinna hi kei, Santuipi in hong khaktan in ka nung uh pan lah Egypt galkapte in hong delh aa mabin laitak zongh hi kei. Canaan gam lut kha lo ding cih ka theih hun zongh hi kei. 
- Pasian a um zolo, a muang zolo mipite ii vangik teng ka puakna mahmah haksa penpen sa ing.
Bang hang hiam cih leh, haksatna tuamtuamte bel Pasian in na lamdang hong piang sak lel in lemlem thei sam hi. 
Pasian zongh a um mello, a muang taklo ahi mipite tawh sunlehzan aa omkhawm, tonkhawm, kithuah cih bel baih lo hi.
- Pasian deihna bang cih zongh kan lo uhin amau ut bek thei uh, tua teng makaih hilai mawk in, tua kawmkal ah, en i upna letkip kul hi sawnsawn hi.
Tua pen kei adingin mi tul tampi lak aa om ka hih hang' hong lunglen huai simtham hi. 
- Ahih hang' Pasian in, hih haksatnate pen kei bek ii puak dingin hong siam lo aa, hong puakpih ding mi hong pia veve hi.

### Interview Part-4 (Moses on Abraham & leadership):
Kei: Pu Moses aw, upna pupi ahi Abraham pen no hunin bang dan aa thei kha na hia?
Moses: Upna pupi baan ah ko ading diakdiakin ko minam ii pupi uh ahi Jacob ii pu taktak hi sitset lai hi.
Egypt kumpi inn sungah ka om hang' ka pu ka pa tangthu pen ka nu in hong hilh sim veve hi. Kam tawh a kigen suksuk hi ung.
Pasian in sawlbawk kuang tawh hong sap ciang, Abraham Pasian- Isaac Pasian- Jacob Pasian hong kici aa kipsakna zongh cian takin ka ngah hi.
Nong dot Pu Abraham pen a kigen thu a thei cih bang bek hilo, ka nuntak khuasakna vuah, ka ngaihsutna vuah a om den khat hi. Tangthu dan bek aa thei hiloin mabaan khatin ka theih khak thu ahi hi.
Pasian in 
- Pu Abraham kamciam pia-in,
- Kei Moses in tua kamciam kalsuankhiatpih,
- Joshua in tua kamciam tangtunpih hi.
Pasian kamciam pawlkhatte, Pasian geelna pawlkhatte pen maw ei bek ii sep ding na hilo hi. Khang khat ai bek zongh na hilo thei lai hi. 
Tun bel, no hun diakdiak ah bang, no mah in sem nuam, no mah in a gah ne nuam thei mawk hi uh teh. Lamdawt khawng kitawn suk ziauziau ta hi. Ngaklah pahpah thei lua hi uh teh.
- Pasian pen Kumpite kumpi ahih manin ama thuthu hi, democracy dan aa ei thu, mipi a tamzaw' deihna a zui hilo hi. 
Tua ahih manin ama deihna thei aa i zuih khak leh, a Kumpi gam sungah thuzui mi, mi hampha, mi nuamsa i kici thei pan ding hi.

### Vocabulary Notes (Tuhun teh a kizangh phialmang kammal pawlkhat):

(1) Thusung = agenda (for meeting); Thului = article (for writing)
(2) Zuha → zu ne (solid form); Zu a tui → zu dawn (liquid form)
(3) Lai-at = writing on stone/tablet; Laikhen = typewriter/computer writing; Laigelh = pencil/pen writing
(4) Galvai: Defence=galdo, Attack=galsim, Conflict=galkido
(5) Van/nate with water: Van items="sawp", Living beings="sil", Rivers="hah", Puddles="phiat"
(6) Ki- compounds: khi→ki-ok, thung-ip→ki-kuah, tau→kibulh, bilbah→kibah, van→ki-pua, vasa→kibeng+kap
(7) No vs neu (size), lian vs gol (big variants)

### Usage Notes (Kammal zat):

Hehnem/Hehnepna vs Dahna/Dahpihna: Old generation used hehnem/hehnepna, now dahna/dahpihna preferred
Gupkhiatna (resurrection): Old meaning "going to better place", now combined with Christian theology

## Grammar Patterns

### Pattern 1: First-Person Narrative with God as Interlocutor

Moses speaks directly to God using first-person (`kei`) while narrating his dialogue:

```
Kei bang Pasian tungah "Bang hangin hih mi teng ii vangik kei tungah hong suan na hiam?"
[1SG] [how] [God] [on] ["Why] [this] [person] [on] [3PL] [kidney] [1SG] [on] [PRFV] [put] [PART] [Q]?"]
```

**Analysis:** The interviewer ("Kei") uses direct quotation of Moses speaking to God. The structure is: `kei bang` (how did I) + `Pasian tungah` (to God) + quoted speech. The quoted speech uses second-person address to God (`na` implied).

### Pattern 2: Quotative Speech with `cih dan`

Direct speech is introduced with `cih dan` (saying manner):

```
"Canaan gam ut lua" cih dan sangin "Pasian Thu ka man loh man hizen aa maw" cih dan hangin ka lungsim a si hizaw veh aw.
```

**Analysis:** Double-quotative structure: `"... "... " cih dan sangin "... "... " cih dan hangin`. The first quote expresses regret, the second quotes Moses' self-reproach. `cih dan` = "saying manner" — the standard quotative.

### Pattern 3: Conditional/Hypothetical with `ci leng`

```
Canaaan gam lut kha lo ci leng, Pasian a muang Joshua leh Caleb bel lut kha-in nang na lut khak loh pen, Pasian na muan loh man a kici thei dia?
```

**Analysis:** `ci leng` = "if we say" — introduces a hypothetical or concessive clause. The main clause then draws a logical conclusion.

### Pattern 4: Rhetorical Question with `diam`

```
Pasian a lungkim mawk mah diam ci aa
```

**Analysis:** `diam` is the rhetorical question marker (not `hiam`). It expresses "how very much" rather than seeking an answer. Structure: `X + diam ci aa` = "X is so much that..."

### Pattern 5: Imperative/Hortative with Softening Particles

```
Ngaih aw. (Think!)
Sem ni. (Do it!)
Hong maisak un. (Please come.)
```

**Analysis:** Commands use softening particles:
- `aw` — soft command
- `ni` — hortative (let's)
- `un` — polite request
- `hen la` — very polite request

### Pattern 6: Ergative `in` with Agent

```
Pasian in Canaan gam lut kha lo ding hong cih ciang bel
Pasian in biakpiakna sang' thumanna deihzaw ahi aw
Mite in attention ngah nuam mahmah uh hi
```

**Analysis:** The ergative marker `in` marks the agent of transitive verbs. Used with: God (`Pasian in`), people (`mite in`), the sea (`Santuipi in`).

### Pattern 7: Future Tense with `ding`

```
Ei pen a lamh ding i hi aa, mi dangte pen a lut ding, ei pen a suan ding
```

**Analysis:** `ding` = future marker. Pattern: `Subject + a + verb + ding`. Used in Moses' prophecy about who will enter Canaan.

### Pattern 8: Negation with `kei` (All Persons)

```
Ka lungdam hi. → Ka lungdam kei hi. (I am not well.)
Heh peuhmah kei ing. (There was no joy at all.)
```

**Analysis:** `kei` is the standard negation particle for all persons. `lo` is also valid but literary/formal. Note: `lo` does NOT take `a` agreement: `Pai lo hi.` (NOT `A pai lo hi.`).

### Pattern 9: Discourse Chain with `Tua leh` → `Tua banah` → `Tua manin`

```
Tua leh... Tua banah... Tua manin...
```

**Analysis:** Three connectors creating logical flow:
- `Tua leh` = "and then" (sequential)
- `Tua banah` = "because of that" (causal)
- `Tua manin` = "therefore" (conclusive)

### Pattern 10: Cleft Focus with `pen`

```
Tua pen khatvei bek cih bang hi kei
Ei pen a lamh ding i hi aa
```

**Analysis:** `pen` = focus/cleft marker. Structures the sentence to emphasize the topic: `Topic + pen + predicate`.

### Pattern 11: Verb Compounds with Directional Particles

```
kalsuankhiatpih (continue + exit)
tangtunpih (pass + on)
phawk sak (understand + give)
zawhpih (end + give)
```

**Analysis:** Zolai verbs are agglutinative. Compounds combine: verb stem + directional/suffix. `-pih` is a benefactive/resultative suffix.

### Pattern 12: Plural Formation with `-te`

```
mipite (people), makaite (leaders), kumpite (kings), galkapte (soldiers)
```

**Analysis:** `-te` is the standard plural marker for nouns. Applied to: `mi` → `mite`, `makai` → `makai-te`, `kumpi` → `kumpi-te`.

### Pattern 13: Possessive with `i` (3PL)

```
Canaaan gam ii nopsakna teng (Canaaan's pleasures)
Mipite ii vangik teng (people's kidneys/minds)
Ko minam ii pupi (my nation's ancestors)
```

**Analysis:** `i` is the 3PL possessive marker. `Noun + ii + noun` = "Noun's Noun".

### Pattern 14: Progressive/Continuous with `ziahziah`

```
Lak ziahziah (taking continuously)
Thugen ziahziah (speaking continuously)
Makaih ziahziah (leading continuously)
```

**Analysis:** `ziahziah` = continuous/progressive aspect marker. Reduplication emphasizes ongoing action.

### Pattern 15: Vocabulary Formation — Water Classification System

```
Van + sawp (water containers)
Van + sil (water creatures)
Van + hah (rivers)
Van + phiat (puddles)
```

**Analysis:** Zolai classifies water-related nouns by compound formation. `van` functions as a classifier prefix for water categories.

### Pattern 16: Ki- Reflexive/Reciprocal Prefix

```
ki-ok (from khi), ki-kuah (from thung-ip), kibulh (from tau)
kibah (from bilbah), ki-pua (from van), kibeng+kap (from vasa)
```

**Analysis:** `ki-` is a reflexive/reciprocal prefix. When added to verb stems, it creates reciprocal or reflexive meanings. Productive morphological pattern.

### Pattern 17: Loan Word Integration

```
Attention (English → Zolai phonological adaptation)
Democracy (English → Zolai phonological adaptation)
```

**Analysis:** Modern Zolai integrates English loanwords with Zolai phonological adaptation. The loanwords maintain their English meaning but are pronounced with Zolai phonology.

## Cultural Notes

### 1. Moses as a Living Figure

The interview treats Moses not as an ancient historical figure but as a living interlocutor. This is a common literary device in Zolai spiritual writing — putting biblical figures into contemporary conversational settings. Moses responds in first person, reflecting on his experiences with the Israelites, his sufferings, and his leadership.

### 2. Leadership and Suffering

The text presents suffering as integral to leadership. Moses' suffering is not physical but emotional — bearing the burden of people who do not follow God. The phrase `Pasian a um zolo, a muang zolo mipite ii vangik teng ka puakna mahmah haksa penpen sa ing` captures the deepest suffering: leading people who do not believe.

### 3. Happiness Through Solitude with God

Moses defines true happiness not as public recognition but as private communion with God: `Sinai mual tungah ae kei leh Pasian bek lian hiveng`. The mountain-top experience — away from people, alone with God — is the peak of joy. This reflects a Zolai spiritual value of intimate divine relationship over public acclaim.

### 4. Democracy and Divine Will

The final section connects divine kingship with democratic governance: `Pasian pen Kumpite kumpi ahih manin ama thuthu hi, democracy dan aa ei thu, mipi a tamzaw' deihna a zui hilo`. This is a sophisticated theological-political reflection: God is King of kings, but His will is expressed through the people's majority will. This bridges traditional Zolai communal decision-making with Christian theology.

### 5. Vocabulary Evolution

The vocabulary notes reveal language change over time:
- **Old generation:** `hehnem/hehnepna` for joy
- **Current generation:** `dahna/dahpihna` preferred
- **Resurrection concept:** `gupkhiatna` shifted from "going to better place" to Christian theological meaning
- This reflects the ongoing influence of Christianity on Zolai spiritual vocabulary

### 6. Water Classification System

The `van + X` compounds (sawp, sil, hah, phiat) reveal a sophisticated classification system for water-related entities. This is a feature of Zolai's agglutinative morphology — creating precise terms through compound formation rather than borrowing.

### 7. Ki- Prefix as Cultural Marker

The `ki-` reflexive/reciprocal prefix is culturally significant — it emphasizes relationships and mutual actions. In Zolai culture, actions are often understood in relational terms (doing to/with each other), and the ki- prefix encodes this worldview grammatically.

### 8. Writing System Evolution

The vocabulary notes distinguish three writing methods:
- `lai-at` (stone/tablet) — ancient
- `laigelh` (pencil/pen) — modern handwriting
- `laikhen` (typewriter/computer) — contemporary

This progression mirrors the technological evolution of writing tools, all expressed through Zolai compound formation.

### 9. Military Vocabulary

The `galvai` compounds (galdo, galsim, galkido) show a systematic military vocabulary. Defence, attack, and conflict are distinguished, reflecting the importance of military concepts in Zolai historical narrative.

### 10. The Bronze Staff as Symbol

The `sawlbawk kuang` (bronze staff) appears as a symbol of divine authority. Moses references it when speaking of God's power, connecting to the biblical narrative of Moses' staff performing miracles.
