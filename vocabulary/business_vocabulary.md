## Cultural Notes

### 1. Property Market in Diaspora

The vocabulary reflects Zomi diaspora engagement with property markets in Myanmar:
- `tul` (price) + `val` (lakh) = property valuation system
- `khe te` (buy) + `le te` (sell) = real estate transactions
- `investment` loan word = modern financial concepts

### 2. Social Media Vocabulary

English loan words dominate social media discourse:
- `facebook`, `like`, `share`, `scam`, `advertising`
- Zolai verbs: `thang` (spread), `ka` (reach), `zui` (follow)
- Reflects digital literacy in diaspora communities

### 3. Values Discourse

The tension between worldly and traditional values:
- `leung paizia` (worldly pursuit) vs traditional values
- `mite zia` (people's ways) vs cultural preservation
- `values` loan word = modern ethical discourse

### 4. Biblical Leadership

Moses' vocabulary reflects leadership concepts:
- `lam` (lead/guide/breathe) = multi-meaning verb
- `ton` (begin/start) = initiative verb
- `cih` (know/understand) = cognition verb
- `sih` (suffer) = experience verb

### 5. Political Systems

Modern political vocabulary integrated:
- `democracy` = people's rule
- `monarchy` = single ruler
- `leadership` = guiding ability
- Contrast between systems explored

### 6. Loan Word Integration

The texts show extensive loan word usage:
- English: `facebook`, `like`, `share`, `scam`, `democracy`
- Technical: `investment`, `inflation`, `property`
- Biblical: `moses`, `israelites`, `pharaoh`, `abraham`
- Zolai maintains grammatical structure with loan words

### 7. Compound Word Formation

Zolai compounds new concepts:
- `kawlgam` = `kawl` (Myanmar) + `gam` (land) = Myanmar
- `leung paizia` = `leung` (world) + `paizia` (pursuit) = worldly pursuit
- `mite zia` = `mite` (people) + `zia` (ways) = people's ways
- `sumdawnna` = `sum` (money) + `dawnna` (doing) = business

### 8. Perfective Tense Usage

`hong` marks completed actions:
- Historical events: `Israelites in Egypt aa a hong om hi.`
- Personal experience: `Ka hong sih hi.`
- Community actions: `Zomite in gam a khe te a inn leh a tul a hong suak hi.`

### 9. Ergative Construction

Agent marker `in` marks transitive subjects:
- `Pasian in a hong lam hi.` (God led)
- `Pharaoh in ka a hong sih hi.` (Pharaoh made me suffer)
- `Diaspora in Zomite a hong siam hi.` (Diaspora created Zomi people)

### 10. Question Formation

`hiam` at sentence end creates yes/no questions:
- `Na in Israelites a hong lam hi hiam?` (Did you lead the Israelites?)
- `Na in lungdam hi hiam?` (Are you well?)
- `Canaan aa a pai ding hi a hiam?` (Will they go to Canaan?)

## LTTuang Vocabulary Notes — Cultural Context

### Document Evolution

The vocabulary shows the evolution of writing terminology:
- **Historical**: `lai-at` (book + write) → classical Zolai
- **Modern**: `laikhen` (book + pen) → contemporary Zolai
- **Contemporary**: `laigelh` (book + write) → modern Zolai

This reflects the evolution of writing tools:
- `at` (mark/scratch) → `khene` (pen) → `gelh` (write)

### Warfare Terminology

The `galvai` (warfare) system shows compound formation:
- `gal` (fight) + `vai` (affair) = warfare
- `gal` (fight) + `do` (protect) = defence
- `gal` (fight) + `sim` (strike) = attack
- `gal` (fight) + `kido` (clash) = conflict

### Water Verbs

Different verbs for drinking reflect nuances:
- `sawp` = neutral drinking
- `sil` = careful/sipping
- `hah` = quick/gulping
- `phiat` = pouring/presenting

### Verb Compounds with `ki-` (REFL)

The `ki-` prefix creates reflexive/mediopassive verbs:
- `ki-ok` = stand up (REFL + stand)
- `ki-kuah` = enter (REFL + enter)
- `kibulh` = fall (REFL + fall)
- `kibah` = change (REFL + change)
- `ki-pua` = appear (REFL + appear)
- `kibeng` = become (REFL + become)

### Size Adjectives

Size descriptors distinguish dimensions:
- `no` = small (general size)
- `neu` = big (general size)
- `lian` = long (length)
- `gol` = tall (height)

### Historical vs Modern Forms

The vocabulary shows language evolution:
- `hehnem` → `hehnepna` (help)
- `dahna` → `dahpihna` (support)

Modern forms add nominalizer `na` or change suffix.

### Resurrection Terminology

`gupkhiatna` (resurrection) combines:
- `gup` = rise
- `khiat` = out
- `na` = nominalizer

This reflects compound formation for theological concepts.
